A reflexive research project

When the framework studies itself.

DIO to DIO examines how different large language models reconstruct and explore Digital Identity Optimization (DIO) and the Ontology of Digital Identity (ODI) when the framework is applied to its own distributed ecosystem.

Independent model analysesGraph-theoretical perspectivesOriginal reports and PDFs

01 / Project

One research object. Independent analytical outputs.

The project uses DIO/ODI as both the subject of inquiry and the conceptual perspective through which that subject is examined. It brings together independently produced LLM analyses of the framework, its concepts, its distributed representations, and the relationships among them.

The analyses are not treated as interchangeable answers to a standardized test. Each report retains its own analytical framing, choice of concepts, methods, graph boundaries, and conclusions.

02 / Research question

How do models reconstruct DIO from within DIO?

How do large language models reconstruct and examine Digital Identity Optimization (DIO) when the framework is applied to itself, and what do their independently developed interpretations and graph-theoretical analyses reveal about the DIO/ODI ecosystem?

03 / Publication principles

Faithful representation, not editorial harmonization.

01

Original Markdown

Each HTML report page is generated from its corresponding original Markdown file. Text, sequence, headings, appendices, and report-specific structure are preserved.

02

Original PDF

The PDF is provided as the original source file, unchanged by the website build.

03

Factual catalogue

Descriptions identify each report and its actual scope. They do not impose common findings, categories, or an interpretation shared by all models.

04

Separate synthesis

This portfolio does not perform a meta-analysis. Any future synthesis would require its own research question, method, and standalone publication.
